Case Study

Hubernyx

Designing a white-label directory platform that lets business associations launch their own branded community — on one shared, maintainable system.

Design SystemsMulti-tenant UXv0 → Figma
Role

UX & Brand Designer, Project Manager

Company

Inside View Global

Timeline

2025 – Present

Overview

Hubernyx didn't start as a product brief — it started as a real, informal problem. Over 700 people were coordinating and discovering local businesses through scattered Facebook groups under the name "Conexión Empresarial," with no central place to browse who was out there, what they offered, or what events were coming up.

Inside View Global saw an opportunity to turn that into a proper platform — but not a one-off build for a single client. The vision was one system that different Chambers of Commerce, Boards of Trade, and business associations could each launch as their own branded directory, without us rebuilding the product every time.

The Challenge

The core design tension was flexibility without losing consistency. Each association needed the directory to feel like their own space — their branding, their community, their events — while the whole thing had to run on one shared, maintainable codebase underneath.

On top of that, I had to reconcile three very different audiences inside the same interaction model: chamber administrators managing the space, business members maintaining their listings, and event attendees just browsing what's available.

Process

Discovery

We didn't run a traditional research phase with formal interviews before building. The need was already validated by real demand — over 700 people were actively living the problem in that original community. Rather than spend early-stage resources proving a need we already knew existed, I prioritized speed toward a working product.

Design

I explored each core use case — onboarding, member profiles, directory browsing — directly through prompts in v0, then took the strongest results into Figma to refine the actual flows and interface before implementation. As the product moved from a single-client tool toward a true white-label model, I built a reusable design system with tokenized branding, so a chamber could apply their own colors and logo without breaking the underlying component structure.

Building the Modules

I designed the event-management and member-directory modules end-to-end — the two pieces that had to reconcile the needs of chamber admins, business members, and event attendees inside one coherent model, rather than three disconnected experiences.

Working With the Team

I worked as UX and Brand Designer alongside our Project Lead and another designer on the visual identity side, and also carried Project Manager responsibilities for the initiative — coordinating scope with developers and go-to-market as the product moved through each phase.

Strategy & Goals

Before any screens, I worked through the project goals and the "why" with the team — framing Hubernyx as a scalable, centralized directory that could serve Vancouver's fragmented business community and position Conexión Empresarial as the go-to hub.

Framing the MVP: what we needed to build, and the problem it solves.

Persona

I built this persona from what we already knew about our early community — not from formal interviews, but from real, lived context: business owners like Sofia were already relying on scattered Facebook groups and word-of-mouth to find clients and connect with peers.

Sofia, small business owner in Vancouver's Latin American community — the core persona behind Hubernyx's design decisions.

Userflow

Mapping both sides of the platform at once: the general user path (discovering and browsing the directory) and the business path (registering and getting a company, organization, or community listed and approved).

User path vs. business path, including the admin approval step before a listing goes live.

MVP Prioritization

With a long list of possible features, I worked through an effort/value matrix to separate what the MVP actually needed — public directory with field masking, multi-entity profiles, approval workflows, admin portal, performance tracking, and the annual renewal system — from everything explicitly deferred (video referrals, event ticketing, payments, mobile apps, and more).

Effort/value matrix used to scope the v1 MVP and explicitly defer everything else.

Reflection

This project taught me how to design for flexibility without chaos — a single system that has to feel bespoke to each client. As Hubernyx moves into larger organizations like the Greater Vancouver Board of Trade, I want to bring more structured research back into the process, especially validating the white-label onboarding flow directly with chamber admins before scaling further.
