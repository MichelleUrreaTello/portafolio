01

One platform, many communities.

Hubernyx — White-label community directory platform, Inside View Global (2025–Present)

Design systems Multi-tenant UX v0 → Figma

The Challenge

Hubernyx grew out of a real, informal problem: hundreds of local businesses were coordinating through scattered Facebook groups, with no central place to connect or find events. Inside View Global wanted to turn that into one platform that different Chambers of Commerce and business associations could each launch as their own branded directory.

The Process

We didn't run a traditional research phase before building — the need was already validated by real demand from over 700 people active in that original community. I explored each core use case directly through prompts in v0, then took those results into Figma to refine the actual flows before implementation. As the product moved toward a true white-label model, the challenge became flexibility without losing consistency: I designed the event-management and member-directory modules to reconcile chamber administrators, business members, and event attendees at once.

Working With the Team

I worked as UX and Brand Designer alongside our Project Lead and another designer, and also carried Project Manager responsibilities — coordinating scope with developers and go-to-market.

The Outcome

Hubernyx went live in December 2025. The pilot reached 65 businesses and 150 active users.

02

Making enterprise deployment self-serve.

Rappid Deploy — AI-augmented enterprise content management platform, Inside View Global (2025–Present)

Enterprise systems v0 → Figma Dev collaboration

The Challenge

Inside View's consulting team had spent years manually deploying Enterprise Content Management environments on Microsoft 365 for clients. Rappid Deploy set out to turn that manual expertise into a self-serve, no-code product non-technical users could operate on their own.

The Process

Validation came from the business itself — 70% of existing consulting clients had already asked for a self-serve version. My job was translating a heavy, admin-dense system (permissions, team management, branding, navigation) into something approachable. I used v0 to explore interaction patterns per use case, then rebuilt those explorations as production-ready prototypes in Figma, working closely with development to keep every screen technically feasible.

The Outcome

Internal validation over 60 days showed strong signals: 10–15% productivity reclaimed.

03

Turning support questions into confident self-service.

Customer Service Chatbot — Rules-based virtual assistant, Despegar Hotel Extranet (2019–2020)

Conversational design Information architecture Metrics-driven

The Challenge

Despegar's hotel providers generated a high volume of support requests across scattered channels on the Extranet. The bot needed to do more than answer well — it had to provide real commercial guidance that supported the business.

The Process

I mapped two user types (beginners vs. experienced providers), built a full content tree per topic — base questions, templated answers, sub-questions, alternate phrasings — and designed flows like "I need to load a promotion" with short, separated responses instead of long text blocks. The result was a rules-based conversational system built around intent recognition, structured decision trees, supporting content, and clear escalation to a human agent when needed.

The Outcome

Live since September 2020: 90% of questions are resolved within the bot, 70% of Q1 interactions were resolved with content and images alone, and the human-transfer rate stays below 10%.

04

Planning a trip before the traveler is ready.

Activity Pass & Dynamic Itinerary — End-to-end product research, Despegar Boarding Pass UX Program (2023)

User interviews MVP prioritization Usability testing

The Challenge

Despegar wanted to shift part of its business from pure booking toward destination advice and inspiration — helping travelers plan the activities around their trip, not just flights and hotels.

The Process

This project ran the full research process: qualitative interviews with real travelers, synthesis of findings (69% said they'd rather spend more on activities than on a nicer hotel), and a prioritized MVP using a MUST/SHOULD framework. From there, I built the information architecture and prototyped "Activity Pass" — a bundle of activities adjustable in date, before or during the trip — then ran usability testing directly on that prototype.

The Outcome

A complete deliverable — functional prototype backed by a documented design system — validated with real research from the initial interview through the final usability pass.

05

Research that changed what the product became.

Nubel — Product Designer for a job-marketplace platform (2024–2025)

Two-sided research Product pivot Video-first UX

The Challenge

Nubel set out to build a hiring platform for two very different users at once: candidates who wanted to stand out beyond a static resume, and recruiters who needed a faster way to evaluate communication and fit. The original concept leaned toward a LinkedIn-style profile with candidate metrics.

The Process

Across interviews, usability tests, and a benchmark, candidates told us they did not trust a smaller platform enough to re-enter information already held on LinkedIn. Recruiters described a different gap: resumes and ATS documents could not quickly reveal communication, energy, proactivity, or language fluency. Those findings changed the product direction to a 100% video-first experience, supported by a candidate portal and companion ATS.

The Outcome

The research changed the product's direction entirely — from the metrics-driven profile concept to the fully video-first experience Nubel is today (verifiable at nubel.ca). The companion ATS was validated with recruiters and used in real sales conversations.
