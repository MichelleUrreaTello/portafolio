Case Study

Rappid Deploy

Turning years of manual Enterprise Content Management consulting into a self-serve, no-code product for non-technical users.

Enterprise Systemsv0 → FigmaDev Collaboration
Role

UX Designer

Company

Inside View Global

Timeline

2025 – Present

Overview

Inside View's consulting arm had spent years manually deploying Enterprise Content Management (ECM) environments on Microsoft 365 for clients — configuring permissions, site structure, branding, navigation, and governance by hand, project after project. Rappid Deploy set out to turn that manual expertise into a self-serve, no-code product that non-technical business users could operate on their own.

The Challenge

The system underneath is genuinely heavy: user permissions, team and organization management, branding, page templates, site structure, navigation configuration. My job was translating an admin-dense, technically dense system into something a non-technical user could configure with confidence — without hiding the power the product needed to deliver.

Process

Discovery

Validation came from the business itself, not from a formal research phase: 70% of Inside View's existing consulting clients had already asked for a self-serve version of what we were building for them manually. That demand signal was the starting point.

Design

I used a two-stage process for every major flow — creating a project, managing permissions, customizing site structure, branding. First, I explored interaction patterns per use case with prompts in v0 to move fast and see options. Then I took the strongest direction into Figma and rebuilt it as a production-ready prototype, refining the details that actually matter for real use: error states, permission edge cases, navigation depth.

Working With Development

Because the underlying system is complex, I worked closely with the development team throughout — checking technical feasibility against every screen before it moved to build, and adjusting flows when a "clean" UX idea didn't map to how the system actually worked underneath.

Outcome

Internal validation over a 60-day period showed strong early signals, and the product moved into a closed beta pilot mixing SMB and enterprise clients.

10–15%Productivity reclaimed

Reflection

Rappid Deploy pushed me to think like a systems designer, not just a screen designer — every flow had a permission model and a technical constraint behind it. I didn't run formal usability testing on this one either; the validation was business-driven. As the beta expands to our signed LOI clients, I want to bring real usability testing into the loop before the next major release.
