Case Study

Nubel

How two-sided research changed a job-marketplace platform's entire direction — from a metrics-driven profile to a fully video-first hiring experience.

Product Designer

Overview

Nubel set out to build a hiring platform for two very different users at once: candidates who wanted a better way to stand out than a static resume, and recruiters who needed an efficient way to evaluate communication and fit at scale. The original concept leaned toward a LinkedIn-style profile with candidate metrics — but we needed to test whether that model created enough value for either side.

The Challenge

The key question was whether another profile-based experience could earn candidates' trust while giving recruiters better signals than a CV. I needed to test the original concept with both sides of the marketplace before investing further in development.

Process

Research

I ran 10 interviews with candidates and HR professionals, five online usability tests with potential users in Canada, and a competitive benchmark. Together, these methods examined what would build trust for both sides of a hiring process that moved beyond the traditional resume.

What We Learned

Candidates did not trust a smaller platform enough to re-enter information already available on LinkedIn. They also found it difficult to locate references from previous work. The skills-visualization concept was interesting, but it did not address the core trust and duplication problem.

Recruiters described a different gap: a CV and the documents managed in an ATS make information easier to review, but do not quickly show proactivity, energy, communication, language fluency, or other soft skills that matter across many roles.

Product Decision

The research changed the direction from a metrics-driven profile to a 100% video-first experience. I defined the scope for a gamified video Q&A mechanic that could give candidates a more direct way to present themselves and help recruiters evaluate communication-oriented signals earlier in the process.

Design

I iterated wireframes for both the candidate-facing job portal and a companion Applicant Tracking System (ATS) for recruiters, keeping both sides of the experience in sync as the concept evolved.

Outcome

The research genuinely changed the product's direction — from the metrics-driven profile concept to the fully video-first experience Nubel is today, verifiable at nubel.ca. The companion ATS was validated with recruiters and used in real sales conversations to gauge purchase interest.

Reflection

This is my clearest example of research actually changing a product's direction, not just refining its interface. The pivot from a profile-and-metrics concept to a video-first experience wasn't a small tweak — it was a different product, and it's the one that shipped. You can see the result live today at nubel.ca.
