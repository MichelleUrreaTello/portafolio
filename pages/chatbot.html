Case Study

Customer Service Chatbot

Building the content architecture for a rules-based virtual assistant that helped hotel providers self-serve on Despegar's Extranet — before escalating to a human agent.

Conversational DesignInformation ArchitectureMetrics-driven
Role

Chatbot Owner & Content Architect

Company

Despegar — Hotel Extranet

Timeline

2019 – 2020

Overview

Despegar's hotel providers — the hotels and partners who manage their listings through Despegar's Extranet — were generating a high volume of support requests across scattered channels. Building this virtual assistant meant combining my UX knowledge with years of prior experience in the commercial department, since the bot needed to do more than answer questions well — it needed to provide real commercial guidance that supported the business.

The Challenge

As the person closest to both the commercial relationship with providers and the product itself, I became the owner of building the bot's content architecture. The goal wasn't just an efficient response flow — it had to provide commercial assistance and guidance that added real value for the hotels using it.

An honest distinction: this was a rules-based, decision-tree assistant, not a generative AI or large language model system. The discipline was the same as any conversational design — understanding intent, structuring a response tree, testing it against real conversations — but the technology itself was structured logic, not generative.

Understanding the Users

To structure the bot's content, I first needed to understand who we were building it for. We defined two user types:

Beginners

  • Need help understanding how to use the Extranet itself.
  • Want to manage the tool correctly to update their information, rates, and availability without issues.
  • Need onboarding for new hires on their team.

Experienced Users

  • Know the tool, but have doubts about commercial topics — destination campaigns, reservations, billing.
  • Want to stay informed about new processes and campaigns.

Content Architecture

Using internal data from different communication channels, plus our ongoing commercial relationship with providers, I identified the core topics the bot needed to cover: content management, payment and billing questions, inventory and rate management, commercial strategy, topics that genuinely needed a human agent, and hotel-industry vocabulary.

From there, I built a content tree for each topic: the frequently asked "base questions" to train the bot on, templated responses, likely sub-questions branching off the first answer (with their own templates), and supporting images, files, and links to redirect the user when needed. For the most common topics, I also mapped out the different ways a user might phrase the same question, so the bot could recognize intent regardless of wording.

Example: The Promotions Flow

One flow started from a real, common request: "I need to load a promotion." From there, I mapped the likely sub-questions and split the responses into short, separate messages — instead of one long block of text — so the user could pick the option that matched their actual question. I also added suggested follow-up flows based on what the user had asked.

Launch & Iteration

The virtual assistant went live on September 14, 2020. In the first quarter, 70% of interactions were resolved with existing content and images — validating the initial content tree. From there, I kept feeding the bot with new flows built from real questions that hadn't had a satisfying answer, and layered in new tooling as I learned what the platform could do: training videos, and triggers for important business announcements.

Outcome

Constant iteration — driven by real questions the bot couldn't yet answer — kept improving performance over time.

90%Of questions resolved within the bot
70%Of Q1 interactions resolved with content & images alone
<10%Transfer rate to a human agent

Reflection

This project is where I actually learned the discipline of conversational design — trees, intents, escalation paths — years before 'AI-enabled UX' was language anyone used. If I revisited a similar problem today, I'd want to explore how a generative, LLM-based layer could handle the long tail of questions a rigid decision tree can't anticipate, while keeping the same escalation principle: automation first, human as the safety net.
